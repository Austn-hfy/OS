## Overview — three phases, not one meeting

What used to be treated as a single "Discovery Meeting" document actually covers three distinct phases of getting a prospect from first contact to an onboarded client:

1. **Pre-Meeting** — software/scheduling side (using the platform's own scheduling tools ahead of the visit). *Not yet defined — see Section 1.*
2. **The Meeting** — the actual in-person conversation, using Rundown. *Fully defined — see Section 2.*
3. **Post-Meeting** — what gets delivered afterward, now fundamentally different from the old two-tier PDF model. *Not yet finalized — see Section 3.*

## 2. The Meeting

**Tool:** Rundown launches the meeting; Grain or Wispr simultaneously records the full audio.

**Core of the meeting, in plain terms:** learning about their hotel, what they currently have, what they want to have, and what needs fixing urgently. Everything below serves those four things.

**Note: `rundown.html` almost certainly needs updating to match this.** Every existing version predates the platform existing as a standalone product — there may still be good fundamentals worth keeping, but don't assume any current version is accurate as-is.

### Stage 1 — Welcome
Comfort, rapport. Personal only — nothing forward-looking, nothing property-specific beyond how long they've been there.

1. So what's your story — how'd you end up in hospitality?
2. How long have you been at this property?
3. Is this your first go at a role like this, or have you been doing this a while?
4. Where are you from originally / what brought you to the area?

**Scripted transition into Stage 2:**
> "So — before we get into all things music, I'd love to just quickly share a bit about what we do, then hear all about you guys and this property."

---

### Stage 2 — About HFY
Goal: answer the unspoken "why are we here" before it becomes a distraction. Not a pitch — just enough to relax into the rest of the conversation. **Delivered as a 3-beat outline, in your own words each time — not word-for-word scripted.** Pause between beats so it lands.

**Beat 1**
> "We essentially serve as an in-house music department for hotels, hospitality groups, and brands."

**Beat 2**
> "We'll work together to figure out what the property should sound and feel like, and then we handle the rest — sourcing talent, logistics, scheduling, payouts, reschedules, even equipment suggestions and maintenance. Pretty much all things music-related, we cover."

**Beat 3**
> "But before we go too deep into that, I'd like to learn a bit more about the property, your guests, and the overall brand."

---

### Stage 3 — Learn the Property
Present-tense only. Organized property → guests → brand (matches the promise made in Stage 2's Beat 3).

**Property:**
1. Tell me about the property layout.
2. Is there anything that really stands out that makes this property different from others?
   - *(If they say no)* → What are some things guests really like? For example, do they like that it's peaceful, or is it more of a communal feel?

**Guests:**
3. What's the typical demographic of guests that stay here — business travelers, families, couples? Younger or older crowd?

**Brand:**
4. What's the property's brand like — more lively and active, or more reserved and quiet?
5. What are some things guests comment on about the property?

---

### Stage 4 — Current Programming
Present-tense only. Needs hard, concrete data, not just narrative color — this is what the post-meeting cost comparison will be built from (see Section 3).

**Schedule:**
1. Walk me through a typical week — which days have music, and what are the hours?
2. Does that change on weekends, or during a particular season?

**Space:**
3. Where does the music happen — same spot every time, or does it move around the property (pool, lobby, restaurant, etc.)?
4. How many spaces total do you have for music, even if they're not actively being used?

**Talent:**
5. Do you guys currently have any DJs or talent you really enjoy working with?

**Satisfaction:**
6. What's working well with the current setup?
7. Is there any good feedback you hear from guests about the music?
8. What's frustrating, or what would you change if you could — is it finding talent, setting up the DJ/audio, keeping track of the calendar, adding to it?

---

### Stage 5 — Anything Urgent Right Now
Opens by directly picking up the thread from Stage 4's frustration question — problem, then urgency.

1. Does any of this require immediate solving?
2. Is there a season or a specific stretch coming up you're trying to plan for — events, residencies, anything like that?
3. Is there a timeline you're working against, or does it happen whenever it happens?

*(Note: the events/residencies question is a real signal toward Special Events — see Business Model Section 3.2.)*

---

### Stage 6 — Their Vision
Future-facing only, all concrete/programming-specific — no abstract emotional framing. This feeds the "what they'd like to do moving forward" part of the post-meeting deliverable.

1. Is there anything you guys have wanted to explore or experiment with in terms of programming?
2. Are there any areas of the hotel that may be lacking energy or revenue that you think music could help with — like an event space or empty area you'd want to activate somehow?
3. Is there any type of event or moment you've always wanted to bring to this property but haven't been able to?
4. Is there anything you'd love to add or change — new spaces, new days, a different feel — if there were no constraints on it?

---

### Stage 7 — Walk the Property
No questions — just the walk. Chip-style checklist:
Guest flow, Transitions, Sound bleed, Equipment, Seating, Bottlenecks, Atmosphere, Branding

---

### Stage 8 — Close
No summary, no live recommendation, no on-the-spot pricing commitment. Genuine thanks + the promise to follow up.

**In-meeting pricing rule:** Never brought up proactively. **The old pricing line here ("$1,500 to $3,500 a month") is now wrong** — current pricing is hourly-based (see Business Model Section 3.1), not a monthly range. If asked in the room, defer rather than quote a stale number until the actual in-meeting language for the new hourly model is worked out — flagged for the same future session as Section 3.

**Close language:**
> "Thanks for your time and all the information — let me put something together for you and your team, I'll send it over via email."

---

### Internal Evaluation — private notes field

A single private notes field, never shown to the client, available throughout the meeting. Used to jot down anything that'll matter afterward — read on the client, how hard to invest in the follow-up, general vibe. No fixed prompts — just open notes. *(The old note about "whether to lead with Complete or Operations Only" is removed — no longer relevant with a single offer.)*

## 3. Post-Meeting Deliverable *(placeholder — needs its own working session)*

**The old two-part PDF (Operations Only vs. Complete) is fully retired** — that comparison no longer exists.

**What's replacing it, in loose/early form — to be finalized:**

The real deliverable now appears to be less like a static document and more like **a live demonstration on the actual platform**, showing:
1. **Their existing programming, organized onto the platform as-is** — "here's what your current setup looks like if you just self-manage it on our system."
2. **What that would cost them** on the platform, self-managed.
3. **What it would cost with HFY taking over some or all of it** — folded into the same view, not necessarily a separate pitch, since there's nothing structurally separate about it anymore (per Section 3.1/Section 4 of the Business Model — a client can mix self-managed and Request-HFY slots on one calendar).

**Explicitly not yet decided:** whether the prospect gets onboarded onto a real account as part of this delivery (i.e., they see their own live account, not a mockup), the exact format, and how this connects to the broader pre-meeting/pipeline process in Section 1. **Aus has flagged this as the next dedicated working session** — this section should stay loose until then, not be treated as settled.
